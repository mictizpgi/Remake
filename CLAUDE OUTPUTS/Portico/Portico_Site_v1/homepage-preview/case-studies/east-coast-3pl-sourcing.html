The challenge

A national consumer wellness products company shipped everything from California, including heavy products bound for the East Coast. That meant long transit times, high freight costs, and more chances for damage along the way. The West Coast warehouse was running out of room, and nobody had a clean, current view of what freight actually cost by lane. Should it add an East Coast fulfillment partner?

Our approach

We framed the decision around cost and speed, with warehouse space as a second benefit, so every option was judged the same way. Then we built the data, the scoring, and the go/no-go case so leadership could decide with clear eyes.

Each step gave leadership something concrete to decide on

  • A rolling year of freight invoices, line by line

    A real baseline: where orders went and what each lane cost

  • Scope limited to outbound, top product line

    A tight scope: returns, quality checks and repairs stayed in California

  • Cost shown beside the capability score

    Price and quality: weighed in the open, not blended into one number

  • A decision deck for the CFO and board

    The full picture: savings, speed gain, risks and timing

The board saw a clear return and chose to wait

A full facility move was about to begin, and taking on both at once would have stretched the team. That was the right call, made with real numbers instead of a guess.

Good analysis doesn’t always end in “go.” Sometimes its job is to show leadership exactly what they’re choosing, and when.

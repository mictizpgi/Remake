Training both sides turned out to matter more than anything else. Both sides of the house started speaking the same language of Lean.

The challenge

Revenue had dipped, mostly with the seasons. The owner saw it as the right moment to invest in fixing the operational issues in the Mexico plant. Leadership and support teams sat in Southern California. The builders were across the border. Two teams, two languages, one operation.

Our approach

We built a Lean training program and ran it twice: in Spanish for the team in Mexico, and in English for leadership and support in California. In the plant, we assessed the floor quickly and Leaned out the facility, removing waste as we went. Then we designed and built a one-piece flow lean cell, so each unit moved from step to step without waiting in a pile.

Everyone was busy, but the floor wasn’t set up for the work to flow

  • On-time delivery was poor, order after order
  • Waste showed up at almost every step
  • Inventory kept piling up in the warehouse
  • Builders spent a lot of their day walking the plant to find what they needed

None of this was a people problem. It was a system problem.
